WAYPOINT #01

Royal Pyramids of Meroë

16.93° N, 33.74° E

The capital of the ancient Kingdom of Kush. Meroë features more than 200 steep-sided Nubian pyramids built over 2,000 years ago as royal tombs for Kushite kings and queens (Kandakes).

UNESCON WORLD HERITAGE KUSHITE DYNASTY

The Two Niles Confluence

Khartoum • 15.55° N

At the heart of Khartoum, the White Nile flowing from Lake Victoria meets the Blue Nile originating from the Ethiopian highlands, merging to form the longest river on Earth.

Jebel Barkal & Sacred Mountain

Karima • 18.53° N

A sacred sandstone mountain rising 98 meters above the Nile valley. Ancient Kushite pharaohs believed it to be the residence of the god Amun and built majestic temples and royal tombs at its base.

5,000 Years of Nile Glory

Trace Sudan's continuous civilization from ancient Kerma to Kush, Meroë, and the modern republic.

2500 BCE – 1500 BCE KERMA KINGDOM

The Kingdom of Kerma

One of Africa's earliest urban civilizations. Famous for its massive mud-brick monument (*Deffufa*), advanced bronze metalwork, and exquisite eggshell pottery.

1000 BCE – 300 BCE 25TH DYNASTY

The Black Pharaohs of Kush (Napata)

Kushite King Piye conquered Egypt, inaugurating the 25th Dynasty. For nearly a century, Kushite monarchs ruled a unified empire stretching from Khartoum to the Mediterranean.

300 BCE – 350 CE MEROË & KANDAKES

Meroitic Era & Warrior Queens

The capital moved to Meroë. Ruled by powerful queen mothers (*Kandakes*) like Amanirenas, who famously defeated Roman legions. Meroë developed its own indigenous written script.

500 CE – 1500 CE MEDIEVAL NUBIA

Christian Nubian Kingdoms

Kingdoms of Nobatia, Makuria, and Alodia flourished along the Nile, building stunning vaulted churches decorated with vibrant Byzantine-style frescoes.

1504 CE – 1956 CE FUNJ & INDEPENDENCE

Funj Sultanate to Independence

The Black Sultanate of Sennar unified central Sudan, cultivating Islamic scholarly traditions. On January 1, 1956, Sudan officially raised its flag of independence.

Living Culture & Generous Ethos

Discover Sudanese hospitality, traditional Jebena coffee, colorful textiles, and rich culinary heritage.

coffee HOSPITALITY CEREMONY

The Jebena Coffee Ritual

Coffee in Sudan is brewed in a traditional clay pot called a Jebena, spiced with ginger and cardamom, and served alongside incense (*Bakhour*). It represents warmth, welcoming guests, and community gathering.

styler ATTIRE & ELEGANCE

The Sudanese Thoub & Jalabiya

Sudanese women wear the iconic Thoub—a 4.5-meter colorful draped garment expressing artistic flair and dignity. Men wear the comfortable, clean white Jalabiya with a matching turban (*Imama*).

restaurant FLAVORS OF THE NILE

Sudanese Cuisine

Kisra (كسرة) Paper-thin fermented sorghum flatbread served with savory stews (*Mullah*).
Ful Medames (فول) Slow-cooked fava beans dressed with sesame oil, feta, and fresh bread.
music_note RHYTHMS & POETRY

Nubian Tambour & Pentatonic Oud

Sudanese music relies on the distinct pentatonic (5-note) scale. Melodic stringed instruments like the Oud and traditional Nubian Kisir / Tambour lyres accompany poetic lyrics celebrating love and country.
